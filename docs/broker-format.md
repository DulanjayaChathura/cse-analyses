# Broker summary format (`broker/<YYYY-MM-DD>.json`)

One file per day: the trading day the posts are about (see CLAUDE.md and `scripts/broker_pending.py`). The "Broker updates" page (`broker.html`) shows them, newest
first, and the build posts the newest one to Telegram once. Raw posts collected
from the Telegram channel are in `inbox/<date>/` (see `scripts/broker_collect.py`).

All numbers are plain numbers. Leave out anything the messages don't give.

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `schemaVersion` | `2` (summaries written before 19 Nov 2026 are `1` and have no `coverage` or `research`) |
| `date` | Summary date (Sri Lanka), e.g. `"2026-11-10"` |
| `source` | Broker name, e.g. `"HNB Stockbrokers"` |
| `posts` | Number of messages summarised |
| `messageIds` | Telegram message ids summarised (from `inbox/*/messages.json`), so nothing is summarised twice |
| `commands` | Ids of the `/summary` command posts this summary answers (see `scripts/broker_pending.py`) |
| `callsPriceDate` | Date of the prices in the broker's recommendation table |
| `note` | Caveats, e.g. figures read from small images |
| `highlights` | 3–5 of the day's most important points, each `{icon, tone, title, figure, text}`: `icon` one emoji, `tone` `up`, `down`, `warn` or `info`, `title` a few words, `figure` the key number (`"Rs 55 → 59.40"`, `"ASPI −0.60%"`), `text` one or two sentences shown when tapped. (Plain strings are still read, for older days.) |
| `coverage` | **Required.** What was read: `[{post, kind, title, items: [{item, status, reason}]}]`, one entry per post summarised (`kind`: `pdf`, `image`, `text`, `link`). `items` lists every section, table and chart in it (each tile, table, chart, paragraph topic); `status` is `included`, `approx` (read off a chart without figures) or `left-out`, which needs a `reason`. `scripts/validate_broker.py` refuses a summary without it, and the site won't publish it. |
| `research` | The broker's research reports (earnings notes, sector or market reports, budget reviews): `[{code, company, report, date, rating, prevRating, target, price, priceDate, keyPoints[], forecasts[], risks[], catalysts[], post}]`. `code` is the full CSE code or `null`; `keyPoints` 3–5 short points (what drove the result, the call's reasoning); `forecasts` their estimates (`"FY27E EPS Rs 12.33"`); `risks` and `catalysts` at most 3 each, as the report gives them. Shown as cards near the top, next to this site's fair value. | A long report (more than about 8 pages) also gets `glance` (3 one-line points shown on the card), `exhibits` (every numbered exhibit label in the report, e.g. `["1a","1b","2a",…]`; `python scripts/exhibits.py <pdf>` lists them) and `details`: sections `[{title, pages, points[], tables[]}]`. Each item in `tables` is one exhibit or panel from the report, titled with its exhibit label and page (`"Capex and capex/revenue (Exhibit 22b, p. 35)"`; a group as `"Exhibits 26a–f"`), and is one of: a table `{title, head[], rows[][], plot, note, approx}`, a list `{title, points[]}`, or a chart with no figures worth tabulating `{title, text, approx}`. Copy printed figures exactly; figures read off a chart start with ≈ and the block has `approx: true`. `plot` says how the page draws a table: `{kind}` with `kind` one of `hbar` (bars across, for comparing companies or items), `vbar` (columns over time), `line`, `waterfall` (start, steps, subtotals starting with "=", end), `heatmap` (sensitivity grids), `range` (low–high per method; `head` Method, Low, High), `riskgrid` (`head` code, risk, likelihood, impact), `scatter` (`head` name, y, x) or `none` (just the table); optional `rows` and `cols` (indexes to plot), `line` (columns drawn as a line on a second scale, e.g. a % next to Rs) and `stack`. Only plot columns in the same unit together. End with a section "Checks on the report" listing anything that doesn't add up (figures that disagree between pages, sign errors). Every exhibit label must appear in a `details` title, or in `coverage` as left out with a reason: the validator checks this.
| `market` | `aspi`, `aspiChangePct`, `aspiPoints`, `sp20`, `sp20ChangePct`, `turnoverBn` (Rs bn), `marketPE`, `marketPBV`, `marketCapTn`, `foreignBuyMn`, `foreignSellMn`, `foreignNetMn` (Rs mn, negative = net selling), `gainers` / `losers` (codes that moved the index), `foreignFlows` (`[{code, mn, approx}]`, `approx: true` when read off a chart without figures), `momentum` (`[{code, pct}]`), `other` (`[{label, value, unit}]`: any other market figure the reports give, such as dividend yield or index points, so nothing is dropped for lack of a field), `heatmap` (`[{code, pct}]`, the heat map's readable tiles) and `world` (`[{name, pct, asOf}]`, foreign indices). Put heat-map moves and world indices in those, not in `other`. |
| `calls` | `[{code, name, sector, price, rating, target}]`. `code` is the full CSE code (`SAMP.N0000`, `HNB.X0000`) so the page can match companies on the site. `rating` as the broker writes it: `Buy`, `Moderate buy`, `Hold`, `Sell`. `target` is `null` when the broker shows TBA. |
| `dividends` | `[{code, name, dps, type, price, yieldPct, xd, pay}]` (dates as `YYYY-MM-DD`) |
| `news` | `[{title, text, topic, figure}]`, short and in my own words. `topic` one or two words (`Economy`, `Banking`, `Policy`, `Energy`, `Exports`, `Tourism`, `Global markets`, `Geopolitics`, a sector name…) for the card's icon; `figure` optional, the story's key number exactly as it appears in `text` or `title` (`"$345mn"`, `"21.8%"`), shown large on the card. |

Example: `broker/2026-11-09.json`.
