CDA to set minimum export prices for coconut products 

22 Nov 2026 | By The Sunday Morning Business Desk 

No outright ban on raw material exports
Minimum prices proposed for shell charcoal, fresh nuts
New pricing mechanisms await Govt. approval

The Coconut Development Authority (CDA) is moving to introduce fixed minimum declared export prices for coconut shell charcoal and fresh nuts in a bid to maximise foreign exchange earnings across Sri Lanka’s coconut export sector. 
Speaking to The Sunday Morning Business, CDA Chairman Shantha Ranathunga said that the authority was pursuing minimum export pricing mechanisms rather than imposing outright bans on raw material shipments. 
A proposal to enforce a fixed minimum export price for coconut shell charcoal has been submitted to the relevant ministry secretary for approval, although the final rate has yet to be announced. 
A separate pricing mechanism is being finalised for fresh coconuts to increase the minimum declared export price from roughly $ 0.40 per nut to approximately $ 0.70–0.80 per nut, targeting shipments to markets such as Pakistan and Dubai where dehusked nuts are exported for cooking.
“The CDA is not going to ban charcoal exports, but we are going to introduce a fixed minimum export price. I have also requested a new minimum declared export price for coconuts to be fixed within two weeks,” Ranathunga said, dismissing earlier speculation that the authority was considering an outright ban on coconut shell charcoal exports. 
The proposed measures are intended to address pricing disparities and retain raw materials for high-value domestic manufacturing. 
Currently, Sri Lankan coconut shell charcoal is exported at roughly $ 300–330 per MT, often to competitors like India, which process it into activated carbon for international markets. Meanwhile, local activated carbon manufacturers face raw material shortages and must import coconut shell charcoal from countries such as Indonesia at around $ 380 per MT.
“We are exporting at a lower price, while our activated carbon manufacturers are importing shell charcoal at a higher price,” Ranathunga said.
By discouraging low-value raw exports, the CDA aims to channel coconut shell charcoal into domestic activated carbon production, which serves high-growth sectors including water purification, air filtration, and electric vehicle battery manufacturing. 
The authority also hopes to redirect fresh nuts towards higher-earning export products such as coconut milk, coconut milk powder, and virgin coconut oil.
The export pricing mechanisms remain pending formal Government approval, with no definitive implementation dates announced.
