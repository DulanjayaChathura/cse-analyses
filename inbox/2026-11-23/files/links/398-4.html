Sinopec retreat affects refinery future plans

View(s):

Sri Lanka’s proposed US$3.7 billion Hambantota Oil Refinery project with China’s Sinopec has stalled prompting the government to seek alternatives, to build a 200,000-barrel-per-day facility.

Energy analysts and government committees point to three primary pathways forward:  to boost Sri Lanka’s foreign exchange earnings through fuel exports, support port and industrial growth in Hambantota, and strengthen energy security.

Energy experts suggest linking refinery development with the China Bay Oil Tank Farm in Trincomalee and the proposed multi-product pipeline from southern India to turn Sri Lanka into a regional storage, bunkering, and refining hub.

Entering a direct Joint Venture (JV) with an incumbent operator like Lanka IOC (a subsidiary of Indian Oil Corporation) offers distinct operational advantages compared to launching a new open-market PPP tender, they pointed out.

The main advantage of partnering with Lanka IOC is the implementation certainty and infrastructure integration as it already operates retail fuel stations across the island and co-manages the Trincomalee oil tank farms with the Ceylon Petroleum Corporation (CPC).

The Sri Lankan government has already screened and shortlisted Expressions of Interest (EOIs) for the modernisation and expansion of the Sapugaskanda Oil Refinery, bringing the proposal one step closer to RFP stage.

In addition to other Greenfield projects including the Sinopec Hambantota refinery proposal that was announced earlier but put off, the state is considering a joint venture project in order to improve its existing refinery infrastructure. After withdrawing from the project due to the government’s refusal to offer the company better market access in addition to tax relief, a Cabinet committee advised the government to cancel the agreement.

It was pointed out that the government would incur no financial penalties, since no formal Letter of Intent (LOI) had been signed between the government and Sinopec.

A  Joint Venture (JV) avoids the exact commercial deadlocks such as disputes over local market entry and supporting utilities that derailed the Sinopec agreement.

However, the open tender for the PPP is ensuring maximum transparency and competitive prices, but the project will take several years before the groundbreaking.

Energy Minister Anura Karunathilaka has stated that two more months were required in order to make the decision regarding the refinery project in Hambantota.
