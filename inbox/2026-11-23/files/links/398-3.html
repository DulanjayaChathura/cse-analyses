Sri Lanka central bank to get marketable bonds next year to mop up liquidity

Saturday November 21, 2026 2:51 pm

Saturday November 21, 2026 2:51 pm

ECONOMYNEXT – Sri Lanka’s central bank will gain a large stock of marketable Treasury bonds starting March next year, giving it a market-based tool to permanently absorb excess cash in the banking system without issuing its own securities, Deputy Governor Chandranath Amarasekara said.

The shift will happen once a temporary “step-down” interest rate feature on the central bank’s restructured government bond holdings expires in March, converting them into standard fixed-rate bonds — known in financial markets as “plain vanilla” instruments — carrying a 5 percent coupon.

“The Treasury bond stock is going to be replaced with plain vanilla instruments from March next year,” Amarasekara said.

“For the remaining maturity, it will be fixed at 5 percent,” he said.

Until now, commercial banks and investors have avoided these step-down bonds because their changing interest rates made them difficult to price.

Once they carry a fixed 5 percent rate, the central bank will be able to sell them at a discount so their yields match market rates, allowing authorities to drain excess rupees after assessing investor appetite.

The bond stock dates back to 2020, when the central bank purchased large volumes of Treasury bills — effectively printing money and cutting interest rates — to close a potential gap in economic output.

During Sri Lanka’s debt restructuring, those Treasury bills were converted into long-term government bonds with lower step-down interest rates for the first two to three years.

Amarasekara was responding to questions on why the monetary authority does not issue its own long-term securities to absorb excess cash instead of raising bank reserve requirements, which act as a de facto tax on commercial banks as they are unremunerated in Sri Lanka. (Colombo/Nov21/2026)

Sri Lanka’s national inflation rises to 41-month high of 8.5-pct in October; transport leads

Monday November 23, 2026 5:40 pm

Monday November 23, 2026 5:40 pm

ECONOMYNEXT – Sri Lanka’s nation-wide inflation index, as measured by the National Consumer Price Index (NCPI) on a year-on-year basis, rose to 8.5 percent in October 2026, up from 8.1 percent a month ago, data from the state statistics office showed.

It stood at its highest since June 2023, official data showed.

The NCPI, an alternative to the widely watched Colombo Consumer Price Index, rose 0.4 percent, in October 2026, registering 225.1 compared to 224.2 in September 2026.

The year-on-year inflation of the food group increased to 7.2 percent in the last month from 6.9 percent in September 2026.

Year-on-year inflation for the non-food group accelerated to 9.5 percent from 9.1 percent a month ago, mainly driven by transport cost after the government raised fuel prices in the end of October.

The NCPI includes 485 items representing consumption of Sri Lanka households. It is compiled three weeks after the end of each month and the release of the key index Colombo Consumer Price Index (CCPI). (Colombo/October 21/2026)

Foreign outflows from Sri Lanka rupee bonds continue for fifth straight week

Monday November 23, 2026 4:20 pm

Monday November 23, 2026 4:20 pm

ECONOMYNEXT – Foreign investors sold Sri Lanka rupee bonds and exited the island nation’s risk-free assets for a fifth straight week in the week ended on November 18, Central Bank data showed, amid depreciation in the rupee currency.

With the latest outflows, the island nation has seen net foreign selling in seven out of the last 22 weeks.

Offshore investors sold a net Rs. 8,583 million (US$26 million) worth of Sri Lanka rupee bonds in the week ended on November 19.

The net selling extended the net foreign outflows from the rupee bonds to Rs. 42.5 billion in the last seven weeks.

Despite outflows in the last five weeks, the island nation has seen a total inflow of around Rs. 29.6 billion into local currency bonds this year through Nov 19, following a net inflow of 71.5 billion rupees last year.

The latest foreign selling comes after the U.S. mid-term election showed strong checks and balances for Trump’s presidential powers and hopes of an end to hostilities against Iran. It also came as the Israel Prime Minister failed to secure a majority in his election.

The rupee had been steady for more than three years before the sharp depreciation in May with the Central Bank citing higher oil and vehicle imports amid a lingering conflict in the Middle East.

The rupee has fallen 6.9 percent through November 13, this year.

Globally, investors are cautious about economic growth due to the impact of the latest Middle East escalation.

The rupee has stabilized after the Central Bank raised its key monetary policy rate by 100 basis points in May to curb inflationary pressure stemming from higher demand. The Central Bank, siting higher inflationary expectation raised the policy rate by 25 basis points on Friday (20).

The island nation has seen an uptick in inflation in the last seven months following a nearly 50 percent hike in fuel prices.

The government raised fuel prices in the last week of October, raising further inflationary concerns.

Before the May rate hike, the Central Bank kept its key policy rates steady since May 2025 after reducing them by 825 basis points over 24 months since June 2023 and foreign investors have been buying rupee bonds. (Colombo/November 23/2026)

Sri Lanka opens draft labour law reforms for public views, briefs lawmakers

Monday November 23, 2026 3:38 pm

Monday November 23, 2026 3:38 pm

ECONOMYNEXT – Sri Lanka has published the first four chapters of a proposed Labour Standards Bill online to gather public feedback as officials move to review and combine the country’s labour laws.

The drafted chapters are now available on the Ministry of Labour’s official website, labourmin.gov.lk, allowing members of the public and relevant stakeholders to submit their views and proposals.

Ministry of Labour officials briefed Members of Parliament on the planned reforms during a special meeting held at Parliament on November 18.

The session was chaired by Minister Anil Jayantha, Chairman of the Ministerial Consultative Committee on Labour Affairs.

An expert committee appointed with Cabinet approval is formulating the new legal framework to update Sri Lanka’s labour rules for the current and future world of work.

Officials told lawmakers that the main goal is to review existing major labour laws and bring them into one updated framework.

The proposed reforms are being developed under four main areas: labour standards, occupational health and safety, industrial relations and disputes, and trade union rights.

Under the new framework, authorities plan to cover paternity leave, maternity protection, and the prevention of violence and harassment in the workplace.

The proposed provisions also address working hours, leave, overtime, making employment contracts formal, and ensuring a safe working environment.

In addition, the laws will cover social security benefits and the rights of part-time employees.

Ministry officials said views from relevant stakeholders—including trade unions, employers, and employee representatives—are expected to be taken into account while shaping the proposed laws.

Deputy Minister of Labour Mahinda Jayasinghe, Members of Parliament, and a group of officials from the Ministry of Labour also attended the meeting. (Colombo/Nov23/2026)

Sri Lanka urged to fill vacant diplomatic posts in US, China

Monday November 23, 2026 3:15 pm

Monday November 23, 2026 3:15 pm

ECONOMYNEXT – Opposition legislator Ravi Karunanayake has urged the government to immediately appoint envoys to several key diplomatic posts which are vacant, including in the USA, China and Germany.

“Why haven’t you appointed ambassadors or high commissioners to Washington, Beijing and Berlin? Seychelles, is a small country alright; then there is Jordan which was recently filled,” Karunanayake asked the foreign minister in parliament.

“Even Canada, we have made no appointment. This is a big question.”
