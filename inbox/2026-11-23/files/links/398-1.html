IMF to consider Sri Lanka’s seventh programme review on December 2

    Colombo, Nov. 22 (Daily Mirror) - The Executive Board of the International Monetary Fund (IMF) is scheduled to consider Sri Lanka’s Article IV Consultation and Seventh Review under the Extended Fund Facility (EFF) programme on December 2, potentially paving the way for the release of approximately US$345 million in funding.

    The review also includes requests for waivers of non-observance of performance criteria, modifications to performance criteria and a financing assurances review.

    The IMF reached a staff-level agreement with Sri Lanka on the seventh review in October. However, the release of funds remains subject to approval by the Executive Board.

    The Board's consideration comes as Sri Lanka approaches the scheduled conclusion of its current US$2.9 billion IMF-supported programme in March 2027, with discussions continuing over a possible follow-up arrangement.

    Analysts have stressed the importance of continued engagement with the IMF to safeguard the country's economic recovery and prepare for substantial foreign debt repayments due in the coming years.

    Sri Lanka's foreign debt repayments are estimated at nearly US$3 billion in 2027 and US$3.6 billion in 2028, when repayments to international bondholders and bilateral creditors, including Japan, China and India, are scheduled to resume.

    Fitch Ratings Senior Director Jeremy Zook has said a follow-up IMF programme could provide a supportive policy framework and a financing backstop amid volatile global conditions.

    He also highlighted the importance of maintaining credible fiscal policies and healthy primary budget surpluses to ensure debt sustainability and support future credit rating improvements.

    A committee has reportedly been appointed to examine whether Sri Lanka should enter into another IMF programme after the current arrangement expires.

    Citi economist Baqar M. Zaidi has said further engagement with the IMF remains the most likely scenario, although a future arrangement could offer greater policy flexibility and more calibrated targets.

    Options under consideration could include a funded Stand-By Arrangement or a Policy Coordination Instrument, he noted.

    Meanwhile, IMF Deputy Director of the Asia and Pacific Department Sonali Jain-Chandra said last month that the Fund stood ready to support Sri Lanka in any appropriate way the authorities wished.

    Speaking at a press conference during the IMF meetings in Bangkok, she said Sri Lanka's macroeconomic situation had improved considerably, with strong fiscal performance and debt restructuring largely completed.

    She also highlighted progress in structural reforms, including improvements in governance, and confirmed that the IMF would continue engaging with Sri Lankan authorities beyond the current review through a possible new financial arrangement, policy advice, surveillance and capacity development.

    Jain-Chandra further indicated that another IMF review mission would take place before the existing programme expires in March 2027.

    President Anura Kumara Dissanayake's third budget, presented in Parliament on November 12, outlined the Government's plans to strengthen the economy following the 2022 financial crisis.

    Sri Lanka's foreign reserves have since recovered to approximately US$7 billion, compared with the severe reserve shortages experienced during the crisis.

    The country has projected economic growth of 4% in 2027 and an average of 4.2% over the next three to five years. However, analysts have pointed out that growth of 6% or higher would be necessary to make a more substantial impact on the country's debt burden.
