UK and Germany campaigns awarded; first German production to go live next week

China, Russia and India promotions await procurement clearance

France, Vietnam and Korea contracts to be awarded next

By Charumini de Silva

Sri Lanka’s Rs. 1.5 billion interim tourism promotion drive is moving into execution across key international markets despite delays, with campaigns in the UK and Germany now awarded and three other markets still progressing through the procurement process.

“The synchronised eight-month digital and public relations (PR) campaigns in the UK and Germany are set to roll out, with the first German market production scheduled for release next week,” Sri Lanka Tourism Promotion Bureau (SLTPB) Managing Director Sanjaya Niroshan told 

the Daily FT.

The campaigns, originally scheduled for September, were strategically aligned with major international travel trade platforms including the WTM in London, which was missed this year, and the ITB Berlin to strengthen Sri Lanka’s visibility among travellers and industry stakeholders in two of its key European source markets.

Each campaign carries a budget of Rs. 100 million and will focus on destination repositioning through influencer engagement, trade promotions, and targeted digital media buying.

As per the SLTPB’s strategy, the overall interim program will span six priority source markets including Australia, UK, Germany, India, China, and Russia from August 2026 to April 2027, with France, Italy, Spain, the Middle East, Scandinavia, Poland, Japan, South Korea to follow. However, given the geopolitical uncertainties in some of the markets, new markets have been considered.

“The procurement process for the seven-month digital and PR activations for India, China, and Russia is now in its final stage,” he confirmed, adding that the Bureau is planning to start awarding contracts for France, Vietnam, and Korea markets immediately after.

The original rollout schedule envisaged the China and Russia campaigns commencing in September and the Indian campaign in October 2026, which reflects the slippage in the execution timetable.

The SLTPB Managing Director said the first phase covering Australia has been underway since August and is scheduled to continue till February 2027. “The Australian campaign served as the initial operational proof point, incorporating digital activations and trade roadshows across Melbourne, Sydney, Perth, and Adelaide,” he added. 

The overall interim strategy is designed to maintain a sustained international marketing presence rather than relying on fragmented short-term advertising bursts. The Bureau’s broader objectives are to generate arrivals to support hotel occupancy and employment, increase daily visitor spending, extend tourism activity into underdeveloped destinations, particularly in the Northern and Eastern provinces, and diversify the country’s tourism offering beyond traditional sun-and-sand experiences.

The blueprint unveiled in August 2026 follows a four-stage conversion approach of identifying target traveller segments, prioritising tourism products, mapping destinations that deliver those experiences, and developing compelling content to convert interest into bookings. The product mix spans leisure, culture, nature, adventure, wellness, luxury, and MICE (Meetings, Incentives, Conferences and Exhibitions).

For execution, the SLTPB has appointed a production agency for campaign films and digital and PR agencies for Australia, the UK, and Germany. Its digital partnerships include Meta and TikTok through authorised sales partners, and Roar Global and YouTube through Aleph Holdings and Yandex for search coverage across Russia and the Commonwealth of Independent States (CIS).

The Market Development Facility (MDF) funded by the Australian Government is supporting local agency oversight, training and development campaign monitoring, and evaluation, while the Digital Marketing Association of Sri Lanka (DMASL) is assisting with campaign optimisation and capacity building.

The digital strategy also seeks to strengthen Sri Lanka’s visibility across emerging travel discovery channels, including artificial intelligence (AI) assistance, generative chatbots, and short-form video platforms, through improved destination data and regional itinerary content.
