Sri Lanka Central Bank raises key policy rate, SRR citing high inflation, credit growth

Friday November 20, 2026 7:54 am

Friday November 20, 2026 7:54 am

ECONOMYNEXT – Sri Lanka’s Central Bank raised its key monetary policy rate by 25 basis points on Friday (20) and increased commercial banks’ Statutory Reserve Ratio (SRR) by 1 percent in a surprise move citing higher inflation and credit growth.

The Monetary Policy Board, at its meeting held Thursday (19), decided to increase the Overnight Policy Rate (OPR) by 25 basis points (bps) to 9.00%.

It also raised SRR by 1 percentage point to 3.00% with effect from December 9, 2026.

“Market interest rates have materially adjusted in response to the monetary policy tightening measures effected in May 2026. Growth of credit to the private sector has continued to slow, although it still remains high,” the Central Bank said in its monetary policy statement.

Markets had been betting on an unchanged monetary policy stance.

The latest move comes amid gradual depreciation in the rupee currency and foreign outflows from the government securities.

“The ongoing conflict in the Middle East has lasted longer than expected. Elevated global energy prices together with rising food prices have continued to fuel inflationary pressures domestically, causing inflation to increase to 8.3% (y-o-y) in October 2026. Core inflation also rose, reflecting spillovers from higher energy and food prices.”

“Global energy prices have eased somewhat recently. However, the second-round effects of previous increases in energy prices are expected to exert price pressures in the near term.”

“Latest projections also showed that inflation could remain higher in the near term and for longer than previously projected, before returning to the target of 5% in the second half of 2027. Inflation expectations, particularly in the one-year period ahead, have also risen recently, although medium-term inflation expectations remain anchored around the target.”

The external sector remained resilient despite uncertainty in the global economy, it said.

“Amid the Government’s debt service payments, continued net foreign exchange purchases by the Central Bank helped increase Sri Lanka’s gross official reserves to around USD 7.1 bn  by end October 2026, while the Sri Lanka rupee remained broadly stable.”

“In view of the elevated inflation outlook in the near term, the Board was of the view that a further tightening of the monetary policy stance would be required to minimise the risk of de-anchoring inflation expectations. Such tightening of policy reflects the Board’s continued commitment to maintaining domestic price stability.”

“Moreover, the buildup of officials reserves through foreign exchange purchases by the Central Bank from the domestic market has resulted in a substantial increase in rupee liquidity in the domestic money market. This could slow the envisaged moderation of credit expansion in the period ahead. Accordingly, the Board also decided to increase the Statutory Reserve Requirement (SRR) by 1 percentage point to 3.00% with effect from the reserve maintenance period commencing 09 December 2026.”

£The Central Bank will carefully assess incoming data on the domestic and global fronts and emerging risks, and stand ready to take measures, as appropriate, to ensure that inflation stabilises around the target, while supporting the economy to reach its potential over the medium term.”

Central Bank Governor Nandalal Weerasinghe early this month told an investor forum that rise in inflation was mainly due to high energy prices and the Central Bank was considering if the monetary policy should be tightened for a cost-push inflation from the supply side. (Colombo/November 20/2026)

Sri Lanka central bank has no credit target, backs borrowing within repayment capacity: Governor

Friday November 20, 2026 5:15 pm

Friday November 20, 2026 5:15 pm

ECONOMYNEXT – Sri Lanka’s Central Bank does not set a specific target for credit growth and is encouraging people and businesses to borrow if they have the ability to repay, Governor Nandalal Weerasinghe told reporters.

Weerasinghe rejected suggestions that the public should delay taking loans, noting that borrowing at current market interest rates supports economic activity when backed by genuine needs, business opportunities, and repayment capacity across sectors.

“At this moment, according to the prevailing interest rates, if one has the ability to borrow, one should definitely take a loan,” Weerasinghe said.

Total credit in the economy is currently growing by more than 20 percent, easing only slightly from around 24 to 25 percent recorded during the 2024–25 post-crisis recovery period.

Weerasinghe noted that sustaining credit expansion above 20 percent for two consecutive years is rare in Sri Lanka’s history and shows that loan supply remains strong.

“Credit growth being over 20 percent means we currently see it as a very good provision of credit to the economy,” the Governor said.

Addressing questions on a recent Central Bank Credit Supply Survey, which pointed to rising loan demand alongside tighter supply, Weerasinghe denied that banks are discouraging lending.

He explained that low interest rates have naturally boosted borrowing demand, while banks must still check each borrower’s repayment ability, project feasibility, and risk under central bank rules before granting loans.

Rather than targeting a fixed lending figure, the Central Bank watches how total borrowing by both the private sector and the government affects overall domestic demand—known as aggregate demand — especially for imports, and whether that demand pushes up inflation.

He added that taking a loan remains each person’s individual decision based on repayment strength, while the Central Bank uses market interest rates simply to adjust overall demand up or down. (Colombo/Nov20/2026)

Sri Lanka stocks close down 0.80-pct after rate hike

Friday November 20, 2026 4:54 pm

Friday November 20, 2026 4:54 pm

ECONOMYNEXT – Sri Lanka’s Colombo Stock Exchange closed down on Friday, CSE data showed, with the benchmark All Share Price Index moving down 0.80 percent.

The ASPI was down 173.40 points at 21,369.72, while the more liquid S&P SL20 was down 1.09 percent, or 67.15 points, at 6,075.28.

Positive contributors to the ASPI were Dilmah Ceylon Tea Company (up 4.91 percent at 1,571.00 rupees), Union Assurance (up 1.75 percent at 69.70 rupees), Nawaloka Hospitals (up 3.48 percent at 11.90 rupees), Richard Pieris and Company (up 0.74 percent at 27.20 rupees), and Hunas Holdings (up 2.68 percent at 15.30 rupees).

Dialog Axiata (down 2.04 percent at 48.00 rupees), Commercial Bank of Ceylon (down 0.58 percent at 214.00 rupees), Hatton National Bank (down 0.82 percent at 393.75 rupees), Laugfs Gas (down 5.83 percent at 45.20 rupees), and Hayleys (down 0.86 percent at 230.25 rupees) were top negative contributors.

Market turnover was 2.33 billion rupees. Health care equipment and services led turnover with 1.01 billion rupees.

Crossings were recorded in Ceylon Cold Stores (97 million rupees) and Hatton National Bank non-voting (54 million rupees), with overall market activity remaining modest outside of crossings, Raynal Wickremeratne, Head of Research and Strategy at NDB Securities, said.

Interest rates were a factor behind the market’s decline, though banking counters fell less than expected, he said, adding that weak global sentiment also kept local investor mood subdued. (Colombo/Nov20/2026)

Sri Lanka market rates rose after May hike: Central bank

Friday November 20, 2026 3:46 pm

Friday November 20, 2026 3:46 pm

ECONOMYNEXT – Market interest rates in Sri Lanka have risen steadily since May 2026, the Central Bank said as it raised its policy rate by another 25 basis points to 9 percent to curb rising inflation.

Following a 100-basis-point rate hike in May 2026, borrowing and lending rates across the financial system adjusted upward, Lasitha Pathberiya, Director of the Economic Research Department at the Central Bank of Sri Lanka, said.

“Since the monetary policy tightening in May 2026 by 100 basis points, we see the adjustment in market interest rates in line with this monetary policy tightening in May,” Pathberiya said.

The interbank call money rate rose by 108 basis points, the one-year Treasury bill rate increased by 146 basis points, and the weekly prime lending rate climbed by 166 basis points since the May tightening.

The rupee has held steady, he claimed, despite global uncertainty from the conflict in West Asia, while central bank dollar purchases lifted gross official reserves to 7.1 billion dollars amid debt service payments.

“The Sri Lankan Rupee remained broadly stable in recent weeks. In the month of November so far, the rupee depreciated by 0.06%, while overall the rupee depreciated by 6.9% so far in 2026,” he said.

The central bank bought around 1.7 billion dollars from the market up to October, with substantial purchases continuing in November.

Because these dollar purchases added excess cash to the banking system, pushing market liquidity to around 450 billion rupees, the Monetary Policy Board also raised the Statutory Reserve Requirement by 1 percentage point to 3 percent from December 9 to absorb 150 billion rupees permanently.

Private sector credit growth has slowed slightly but remains high at 21.8 percent, while headline inflation reached 8.3 percent in October due to higher energy and food costs. (Colombo/Nov20/2026)

LankaPay reports security incident at LankaSign registration portal

Friday November 20, 2026 2:46 pm

Friday November 20, 2026 2:46 pm

ECONOMYNEXT – Sri Lanka’s national payment network operator, LankaPay (Pvt) Ltd, said a data collection portal used to register for its LankaSign service recently experienced a ‘security incident’ that may have compromised users’ personal details.

In a notice signed by Deputy Chief Executive Officer Dinuka Perera, the firm stated that the breach potentially exposed personal information including names, National Identity Card (NIC) numbers, email addresses, and mobile numbers.

LankaPay said the incident was strictly confined to the registration data collection portal and did not affect any core infrastructure.

“This incident is confined only to the above data collection portal, and no other systems including payment systems or digital signature platforms have been affected or impacted,” the notice stated.

The company said it has taken appropriate steps to contain the breach and is monitoring the situation.

Affected users or those seeking further clarification have been advised to contact LankaPay’s Data Protection Officer, Lakmal Uyanahewage, via email at dpo@lankapay.net.

Regulated by the Central Bank of Sri Lanka, LankaPay operates the country’s national payment network and serves as a certification authority issuing digital signatures through its LankaSign platform. (Colombo/Nov20/2026)

Fitch assigns Asia Asset Finance’s Rs3bn debt ‘A-(lka)’ rating

Friday November 20, 2026 2:10 pm

Friday November 20, 2026 2:10 pm

Fitch Ratings – Singapore: Fitch Ratings has assigned Asia Asset Finance PLC’s (AAF, A+(lka)/Stable) proposed Sri Lankan rupee-denominated subordinated listed debentures of up to LKR3 billion a final National Long-Term Rating of ‘A-(lka)’.

The proposed debentures will have a maturity of five years and will be listed on the Colombo Stock Exchange. The company plans to use the proceeds to strengthen its Tier 2 capital base and support loan-book expansion.

The final rating is the same as the expected rating assigned on 26 October 2026 and follows the receipt of documents conforming to information already received.

Key Rating Drivers

The proposed subordinated debentures are rated two notches below AAF’s National Long-Term Rating. This reflects our baseline notching for loss severity for this debt class and our expectation of poor recoveries in the event of default.

We applied our Bank Rating Criteria to rate the proposed debentures, as we view the prudential capital framework of Sri Lankan finance companies as closer to that of banks. There is no additional notching for non-performance risk, as the proposed debentures do not contain going-concern loss-absorption features.

AAF’s National Long-Term Rating of ‘A+(lka)’ with Stable Outlook reflects our expectation that India-based Muthoot Finance Ltd (MFL, BB+/Stable) would provide extraordinary support to its Sri Lankan subsidiary, if required. This view takes into account MFL’s ability to support AAF, underpinned by the parent’s standalone profile as India’s largest gold-backed lender. We also consider MFL’s 78.4% shareholding in AAF, record of ordinary equity support and its strategic and operational alignment with AAF in gold-backed lending.

Even so, AAF’s strategic importance to MFL is constrained by its small size, modest group role, low contribution, limited brand sharing and operations in a different jurisdiction. AAF’s standalone credit profile does not drive its rating, but we assess it as significantly weaker than its support-driven rating.

See our commentary, Fitch Affirms Asia Asset Finance at ‘A+(lka)’; Outlook Stable for details on AAF’s key rating drivers and sensitivities.

RATING SENSITIVITIES

Factors that Could, Individually or Collectively, Lead to Negative Rating Action/Downgrade
A downgrade of AAF’s National Long-Term Rating would lead to a downgrade of the subordinated debt rating.

Factors that Could, Individually or Collectively, Lead to Positive Rating Action/Upgrade
An upgrade of AAF’s National Long-Term Rating would lead to an upgrade of the subordinated debt rating.
