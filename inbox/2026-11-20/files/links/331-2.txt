Budget 2027: ‘SL needs specificity on trade reform, IMF’: ODI

20 Nov 2026 | By Nethmi Rajawasam

While Sri Lanka’s 2027 Budget demonstrates its effort to prioritise macro-economic stability, productivity, export promotion, investment, digitalisation and state capacity – the lack of specificity on trade integration, labour market reform, and the Sri Lankan government’s continued work with the IMF leave more to be desired, Overseas Development Institute Global (ODI) said in its review of the Budget.

Commenting on the much expected key Budget priorities, the international development think-tank noted that the Government’s Budget aligns with its own growth report, formulated with local think tank, the Centre for Poverty Analysis (CEPA), comprising 30 proposals.

“The 2027 budget strongly aligns with the broader trajectory of the ODI Global and CEPA growth report, which outlined 30 proposals to sustain growth through 2030. While the budget embraces many of the report's recommendations on macroeconomic stability, productivity, export promotion, investment, digitalisation and state capacity, greater specificity is needed regarding trade integration, labour-market reform, working with the IMF and capacity-building within the state.”

The think tank recommended that Sri Lanka, while deepening its goods-only FTA with India – also learn from its more recent breakthrough FTAs signed with the United Kingdom, and the conclusion of its negotiations with the European Union, that is pending signing. The success of the FTAs had been arrived at after years of aggressive pursuit on part of the Indian Government, as noted by the ODI. 

“The budget also prioritises the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP), a new FTA with China, and Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreements with the United Arab Emirates and Oman.”

“While this is good progress, the global trade landscape is shifting rapidly. With world trade projected to outperform IMF and WTO forecasts in 2026, and India aggressively pursuing FTAs with the EU and the UK, Sri Lanka must inject urgency into its trade agenda and rapidly build its institutional negotiation capacity.”

“A key recommendation from our recent research is to evolve the current goods-only India-Sri Lanka FTA into a deeper, comprehensive agreement. Encouragingly, the budget signals a renewed commitment to regional integration,” the think-tank said, welcoming the reiterated efforts to pursue the comprehensive FTA with India, and the appointment of an official committee tasked with it.

Notably, the ODI also suggested that Sri Lanka must consider establishing its own independent growth commission, following the examples set by South Korea, Malaysia, Singapore, Vietnam and India, to formulate and insulate long-term national growth plans from inconsistent implementation.

“Establish an independent growth commission: Drawing on successful models from South Korea, Malaysia, Singapore, Vietnam and India, this body would formulate and insulate a long-term national growth plan.”

Deputy Finance Minister Anil Jayantha Fernando, speaking to Ada Derana recently said that Sri Lanka is likely to enter into another International Monetary Fund agreement, following the end of the $2.9 billion Extended Fund Facility (EFF) programme in March 2027.
