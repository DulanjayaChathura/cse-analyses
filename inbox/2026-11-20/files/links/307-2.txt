Demand index rises to 42.8, overtaking willingness to lend, reversing 2Q

Willingness to lend to SOEs plunges to -78.7, while SOE demand outlook tops all sectors at 64.8

NPL index falls to -30.1, reversing 2Q  rise in corporate, SME, SOE bad loans

Sri Lanka’s banks became markedly more cautious about lending in the third quarter even as demand for loans picked up, the Central Bank’s latest Credit Supply Survey showed. Their outlook for the final quarter points to a wider gap between the two. The survey comes ahead of the Central Bank’s monetary policy announcement this Friday (20).

The survey covers all licensed commercial and specialised banks. It is built on diffusion indices, which run from -100 to 100 and are weighted by each bank’s share of the sector’s total loan book. A positive reading means an increase, and the size of the number shows how widespread the change is.

Overall willingness to lend fell to 27.4 in the third quarter from 40.4 in the second. The reading is still positive, so willingness rose, but at a much slower pace. The slowdown showed up in retail (38.0 from 52.0), corporate (41.4 from 43.9) and SME lending (25.3 from 27.8).

“This overall slowdown could largely be attributed to the gradual transmission of the monetary policy tightening measures introduced in May 2026, coupled with more selective risk appetite of the banks, and regulatory and exposure limitations related constraints,” the Central Bank said.

Demand moved the other way. The demand-for-loans index rose to 42.8 from 35.1, with increases across all four sectors. This puts the demand index ahead of the willingness index for the first time since the first quarter, after willingness (40.4) had led demand (35.1) in the second.

The fourth-quarter outlook widens that gap. Willingness to lend is expected at 10.4, the lowest of the five quarters the survey covers, against expected demand of 35.1.

Corporate lending shows the widest split. Expected corporate demand rises to 52.2, while willingness to lend to corporates falls to 8.6 from 41.4. Willingness to lend to state-owned enterprises (SOEs) dropped to -78.7 in the third quarter from 1.4 in the second. The outlook for SOEs is -54.7, even as expected SOE demand, at 64.8, is the highest of any sector. Official data show credit to public corporations and State-Owned Business Enterprises fell by Rs. 16.9 billion to Rs. 462.9 billion in September, with the year-on-year contraction widening to 24% from 22.3% in August. 

“This slowdown is largely attributable to the anticipated inflationary pressures, rising operating costs, and restrictions on leasing facilities,” the Central Bank said of banks’ fourth-quarter outlook on willingness to lend.

Banks expect loan demand to keep rising in all four sectors in the fourth quarter. In retail, though, the demand index is expected to fall to 13.9 from 50.0, so retail demand will rise far less widely than before.

“The anticipated increase is primarily attributable to higher import financing requirements, seasonal demand, increased working capital needs to manage higher operating costs associated with inflationary pressures, and the expected improvement in overall economic activities,” the survey said.

Asset quality improved. The non-performing loan (NPL) index fell to -30.1 from -3.8, with NPLs declining in all four sectors, reversing the second-quarter rise in the corporate, SME and SOE sectors. The Central Bank credited accelerated recovery actions and remedial options, improved portfolio performance, and rescheduling resulting in stage upgrades. Loan rejections fell to -11.7 from -0.3. The decline came in the retail, corporate and SME sectors, while SOE rejections were broadly unchanged.

The findings fit other recent signs that credit growth is cooling. Year-on-year private credit growth slowed for a third straight month to 23.6% in September, from 24.5% in August and 26.4% in July. The banking sector’s credit-to-deposit ratio rose above 70% for the first time in three years, reaching 74.0% at end-June 2026. The Central Bank raised the Overnight Policy Rate by 100 basis points to 8.75% on 26 May, 2026. 

For Friday’s decision, the survey points both ways. The Central Bank links the slowdown in banks’ willingness to lend to the May tightening. At the same time, banks expect inflationary pressures and higher import financing needs to drive loan demand in the fourth quarter.
