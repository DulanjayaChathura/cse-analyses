Fitch thumbs up for Budget

Ratings agency says NPP Budget 2027 preserves debt reduction path 

Says Budget 2027 broadly aligned with IMF program parameters, and likely to support Seventh EFF Review

Sees potential for further capex under-execution, which could drive slightly higher surplus

Notes ‘politically difficult trade-offs required’ to comply with 13% expenditure ceiling under PFMA

Warns fiscal vulnerabilities remain high; says fiscal policy credibility critical to future rating momentum

President and Finance Minister Anura Kumara Dissanayake delivering the 2027 Budget speech in Parliament last week (12)

Fitch Ratings yesterday gave Budget 2027 a major vote of confidence, saying it signals the Government’s ongoing commitment to fiscal discipline and debt reduction while accommodating higher spending on investment and cyclone reconstruction.

For a country that long grappled with twin deficits, a primary surplus and falling debt are no small gain, and coming as Parliament debates Budget 2027, Fitch’s assessment gives investors and lenders an independent signal that the hard-won adjustment of the past few years is working, even though the road ahead is long and painful trade-offs remain 

“Fiscal discipline, demonstrated again by the outperformance of fiscal targets in 2026, and a focus on debt reduction were key factors behind our upgrade of the sovereign rating to ‘B-‌’/Stable from ‘CCC+’ in September,” the rating agency said in a statement yesterday.

It noted the Budget targets a primary surplus of 2.3% of GDP, down from an estimated 3.6% in 2026. “The 2026 surplus is well above the 2.5% target in last year’s budget and our earlier 2.6% forecast, reflecting stronger revenue. The decline in the surplus in 2027 reflects lower revenue and higher capital spending but remains consistent with a debt reduction path. This implies a 2027 deficit of 4.5% of GDP, wider than the 3.7% in 2026,” Fitch said.

It noted that the Budget assumptions appear credible and broadly align with Fitch Ratings’ forecasts from its September sovereign review. “The 2027 primary-surplus target is slightly below our 2.8% forecast because planned spending is higher than we assumed. However, we think there is potential for further under-execution on capex again this year, which could drive a slightly higher surplus”.

Primary expenditure is budgeted at 13.6% of GDP in 2027, above the 13% statutory ceiling under the 2024 Public Finance Management Act (PFMA). The temporary deviation from the PFMA mainly reflects capital spending for reconstruction following Cyclone Ditwah, as well as higher public capex plans. The plans include Rs. 260 billion carried forward from a supplementary budget for cyclone reconstruction passed in December 2025 but not spent in 2026 as a result of under-execution. 

“This is the second year the Government has sought a deviation from the PFMA, although budget under-execution meant the ceiling was not actually breached in 2026,” Fitch noted.

The ratings agency said sustaining recent revenue gains will help underpin strong primary surpluses. Revenue is budgeted to moderate to 15.8% of GDP in 2027 from 16.6% in 2026 and 16.7% in 2025, as the boost to import-duty collections from pent-up vehicle-import demand fades as imports normalise. However, efforts to broaden the tax base and improve compliance are supporting more broad-based revenue improvements.

“The budget is broadly aligned with International Monetary Fund program parameters. This should support the seventh review of Sri Lanka’s Extended Fund Facility. The current program ends in March 2027, and the Government announced the establishment of a committee to assess a potential follow-on arrangement, which could provide a further policy anchor,” Fitch said.

Sustained efforts to cement revenue gains and expenditure restraint will remain central to achieving ongoing debt reduction and for the future path of the sovereign rating. Over the medium term, the Government aims to maintain a primary surplus of 3.0% of GDP, requiring politically difficult trade-offs to comply with the 13% expenditure ceiling under the PFMA. Efforts to boost investment spending could help lift potential GDP growth, which we forecast at 4.2%, and support steady fiscal outcomes. However, this will depend on capex execution, effective targeting and progress on broader structural reforms.

Fitch warns fiscal vulnerabilities remain high despite the substantial fiscal adjustment achieved in the past few years. 

“We forecast Government debt will drop to about 92% of GDP in 2026, from 115% in 2022, but well above the 55% median for ‘B’ rated sovereigns. We expect interest payments to decline to around 41.0% of revenue in 2026 from nearly 80% in 2023, but remain more than three times the 12.7% ‘B’ median. A lengthening record of fiscal policy credibility and healthy primary surpluses will likely be critical towards ensuring debt sustainability and supporting potential future rating momentum,” Fitch observed.
