SLT-Mobitel to upkeep institution-matched salaries 

19 Nov 2026 | By Nethmi Rajawasam

With ambitions to invest in its workforce, Sri Lankan telecommunications State-owned enterprise SLT-Mobitel is to continue to pay its employees recently institution-matched salaries – which is likely to cost a ‘few billions’ each year, according to Chairperson Mothilal de Silva. He made these comments speaking at an event announcing the company’s financial performance on Tuesday (18).
Describing the process of salary benchmarking, de Silva said: “Since last October, we have taken concrete steps to invest in our people; correcting salary anomalies for Non-Executives, unprecedented bonus and salary increments at the beginning of this year, increasing meal and transport allowances, and conducting a salary survey. This year we used a third party global consultant to do that. They surveyed the salaries of different categories in Sri Lanka, and we tried to benchmark that.” 
De Silva added that the culmination of the efforts led to increases in salaries in the month of October. “We increased the salaries for all employees last month, based on that report. We tried to match the salaries of other institutions in Sri Lanka. These new benefits will cost us at least a few billion per year, but we believe this is an investment, not an expense.”
According to its Q3 financials, the Group’s profit after tax during the first 9 months of the year increased by 59.4% to Rs 10.03 billion, with a revenue growth of 11.1%. 
During a previous announcement of its H1 financials, CEO Riyaaz Rasheed noted that though the Group saw an 11.1% revenue growth in H1 2026, and Rs 6.1 billion increase in revenues, it also experienced an elevation of operational costs – driven by the volatile global economic and trade headwinds.
Four years ago, the Sri Lankan Government had expressed its intention to divest its majority stake in the SOE in the national budget statement for 2023, as part of a broader policy to restructure and reform loss-making State-Owned Enterprises (SOEs) amid the country’s severe 2022 economic crisis.
Though the process had entered the stage of welcoming private sector bidders, the divestiture was scrapped as the current administration opted to retain ownership of the SOE as a national asset – a decision that aligned with the Sectoral Oversight Committee’s recommendation against privatisation on national security grounds.
Further, de Silva added that the SOE is to diverge from its telecommunications focus to gradually transform into a technology focused entity. “We are transforming from a telecom company into a technology company. We are building a digitised telecom, which is a long journey we are taking step by step.  It won’t be a fully-fledged technology company yet, we will be a digitalised company releasing digital products, and moving away from the connectivity side.”
He maintained that while the connectivity front of the business is its main driving momentum,  its competition from over-the-top service providers necessitate the transition towards becoming tech-focused.
“The connectivity business will be the livewire for a long period to come, and we will gradually move into this technology space. We are accelerating it, because we get a lot of competition, not from other telecom operators, but from over the top providers. In order to survive the market, we have to move into digital products, and we have to move into areas that we have not explored before, like AI. We have introduced this token, tokens will be the next data,” he said.
