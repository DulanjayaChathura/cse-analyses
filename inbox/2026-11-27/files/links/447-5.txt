Section 19A waivers make up 91% of total at Rs. 131 b as fuel imports rise

Wider count brings petrol and bamboo sticks into 19A estimates; 2024 figures revised

Animal feed ingredients get Rs. 7.8 b in relief; relief on CEB machinery falls to Rs. 1.1 b

Rs. 74 b forgone in 1H 2026, with 19A waivers making up 89.8%

Free Trade Agreement tariff cuts and export processing schemes left out of estimates

Customs Import Duty (CID) reliefs rose 4.4% to Rs. 144 billion in 2025 from Rs. 138 billion in 2024, driven by duty waivers on fuel imports, the Finance Ministry’s Tax Expenditure Statement (TES) 2025/2026 showed.

Waivers granted for general purposes and special projects under Section 19A of the Customs Ordinance accounted for Rs. 131 billion, or 91% of the total. The statement describes these waivers as covering consumer goods, to improve living standards, and special infrastructure projects that contribute to economic development. 

They rose by Rs. 7.1 billion, or 5.7%, from Rs. 124 billion in 2024, as combined petrol and diesel imports increased 5.4% to 2.51 million metric tons from 2.38 million metric tons.

Part of the increase reflects a broader count. “The CID expenditure related to Section 19A general waivers was limited to diesel and wheat grain products in the TES 2024/2025. However, the CID expenditure related to Section 19A general waivers was extended to cover products including petrol and bamboo sticks, together with other products identified in the Customs database, in the TES 2025/2026,” the Ministry said, adding that the 2024 data were revised accordingly.

Exemptions on ingredients other than maize, lentils, and rice used to manufacture animal feed were the second-largest item at Rs. 7.8 billion, or 5.4% of the total, down from Rs. 9.2 billion in 2024. Exemptions on machinery and equipment for the Ceylon Electricity Board (CEB) and its subsidiaries fell to Rs. 1.1 billion from Rs. 1.8 billion.

Medicine-related exemptions rose to Rs. 1.4 billion from Rs. 1.3 billion. These comprised Rs. 870 million on products registered with the National Medicines Regulatory Authority (NMRA), Rs. 495 million on raw and packing materials for pharmaceutical manufacturing, and Rs. 12 million on raw materials for Ayurvedic medicine. Materials for agricultural purposes received Rs. 394 million in relief and raw materials for sanitary napkins Rs. 56 million, down from Rs. 78 million.

In the first half of 2026, CID reliefs amounted to Rs. 74 billion. Section 19A waivers accounted for Rs. 67 billion, or 89.8%, followed by animal feed ingredients at Rs. 4.9 billion, or 6.6%. CEB-related exemptions fell to Rs. 179 million, or 0.24%.

The estimates are measured against the standard tariff applied to all imports on a Most Favoured Nation basis. That standard excludes concessions under Board of Investment (BOI) agreements, Free Trade Agreements, and project-specific exemptions. “Although the tariff structure may contain commonly applied rates, the applicable rate varies according to the commodity and tariff classification,” the Ministry said.

Several Customs concessions are treated as part of the normal structure of the tax and are therefore excluded from the estimates. These include reduced tariffs under Free Trade Agreements, duty-free imports into bonded warehouses for re-export, materials imported for export processing under the Temporary Import for Export Processing (TIEP) and Investor Facilitation Centre (INFAC) schemes, and imports by diplomatic missions. “Not every provision that reduces tax relative to a theoretical or simplified tax base constitutes a tax expenditure,” the Ministry said.
