The World Bank Group is expanding access to longer-term Sri Lankan rupee financing to help businesses reduce foreign exchange risk and enabling new private investment in renewable energy and infrastructure.

 In a first of its kind transaction in Asia, the World Bank Group Treasury has arranged a six-year Sri Lankan rupee (LKR) borrowing line between the International Finance Corporation (IFC), a member of the World Bank Group focused on the private sector, and HSBC Sri Lanka. The fixed-rate facility, equivalent to $ 5 million, is the IFC’s first borrowing line in Sri Lanka.

The facility is already helping mobilise longer-term financing for renewable energy in Sri Lanka. Combined with additional local currency funding arranged by the World Bank Group Treasury, it supports the IFC’s $ 10 million, 10-year rupee-denominated loan to one of Sri Lanka’s largest renewable energy companies. The investment is the IFC’s longest-tenor rupee loan to date, its first rupee-denominated infrastructure loan, and its first infrastructure investment in Sri Lanka since the country’s recent economic crisis.

 By raising rupees locally, the World Bank Group can lend in the currency businesses earn in, reducing their exposure to exchange-rate risks. Building on this first facility, the World Bank Group aims to expand long-term local currency financing in Sri Lanka to support private investment and growth.

World Bank Group Treasury Director Client Solutions and Structuring Keshav Gaur said:  “Access to long-term local currency financing can help businesses invest, grow and create jobs without taking on foreign exchange risk they may not be able to manage. This first borrowing line in Asia expands the financing solutions we can bring to emerging markets, helping channel private investment to sectors that can drive growth and economic opportunity.”

The transaction forms part of HSBC’s broader partnership with the World Bank Group in Sri Lanka and across HSBC’s global network. HSBC participates in the World Bank Group’s Private Sector Investment Lab to identify new avenues for public-private collaboration and mobilise private capital to support sustainable development.

HSBC Sri Lanka CEO Mark Surgenor said: “We are proud to support this milestone partnership with IFC, which expands access to longer-term Sri Lankan rupee financing for companies and helps them manage foreign-exchange risk. By combining HSBC’s local expertise and global capabilities with the IFC’s development mandate, we look forward to exploring further practical solutions that support Sri Lankan businesses and the country’s longer-term priorities.”

The Sri Lanka facility builds on borrowing lines established by the IFC and Citi in Kenya in 2024 and South Africa in 2026. Over the past decade, IFC has committed more than $ 33 billion in local currency financing across 71 currencies, helping businesses access longer-term financing in markets where it is often limited.
