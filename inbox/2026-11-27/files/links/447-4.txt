Ministry says figures are conservative minimum as returns-based data misses unreported supply

No cost estimates for individual exemptions on wheat, medicine and electricity

Value Added Tax (VAT) and Social Security Contribution Levy (SSCL) reliefs cost Sri Lanka Rs. 636 billion in 2025, and the Finance Ministry warned that the true figure is likely to be higher.

The Ministry’s Tax Expenditure Statement 2025/2026 shows VAT reliefs rose 25.8% to Rs. 419 billion in 2025 from Rs. 333 billion in 2024, an increase of Rs. 86 billion. SSCL reliefs amounted to Rs. 216 billion. VAT is charged at a standard rate of 18%, while SSCL is levied at 2.5% of liable turnover.

Non-Board of Investment (BOI) companies, excluding Strategic Development Projects (SDPs) and Port City projects, accounted for Rs. 391 billion of VAT relief and Rs. 200 billion of SSCL relief. Among them, the wholesale and retail sector received Rs. 188 billion in VAT relief and Rs. 54 billion in SSCL relief. 

Electricity and gas received Rs. 65 billion in VAT relief, other manufacturing activities Rs. 47 billion, and construction Rs. 38 billion. In SSCL, other manufacturing activities received Rs. 20 billion, textiles and garments Rs. 16 billion and transport Rs. 14 billion.

The trend continued into 2026. VAT reliefs reached Rs. 243 billion in the first half, 17.2% higher than the Rs. 207 billion recorded a year earlier, while SSCL reliefs amounted to Rs. 135 billion. Wholesale and retail again led VAT reliefs for non-BOI companies at Rs. 113 billion, followed by electricity and gas at Rs. 36 billion. Construction fell to Rs. 8.2 billion.

The statement disclosed one new VAT relief: an exemption on liquid milk and yoghurt made from locally produced fresh milk, effective from 11 April 2025.

However, the Ministry said the estimates understate the full cost. “The VAT and SSCL estimates are calculated based exclusively on tax returns filed by registered taxpayers. This represents a major limitation, as it fails to capture the true size of the country’s actual or potential economic supply,” the statement said.

Measuring the full cost would require national supply and use tables and detailed consumption data, which the Ministry said are not fully available. “Because we cannot measure the gap between total potential economic supply and what is formally reported, these VAT and SSCL expenditure figures should be viewed as a conservative minimum rather than the absolute total of foregone revenue,” it said.

The statement’s list of individual VAT and SSCL exemptions, which covers items ranging from wheat flour, infant milk powder and pharmaceuticals to electricity, tea and tourism services, carries no cost estimates for 2024, 2025 or the first half of 2026. The only items with figures are two Port City VAT reliefs, both for 2024 only. “Where a provision is identified as a potential tax expenditure but cannot be reliably quantified, it is disclosed separately rather than presented with an unsupported estimate,” the Ministry said.
