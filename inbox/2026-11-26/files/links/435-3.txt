SriLankan Airlines adds fuel surcharge to break even by March after $35mn first-half loss

Wednesday November 25, 2026
 4:43 pm

Wednesday November 25, 2026
 4:43 pm

ECONOMYNEXT – SriLankan Airlines has added a small fuel surcharge to upcoming ticket sales in a bid to break even by March, after surging jet fuel prices pushed the national carrier to a 35 million US dollar loss in the first two quarters of the year, Deputy Minister of Ports and Civil Aviation Janitha Ruwan Kodithuwakku said.

Speaking on Derana’s Big Focus programme, Kodithuwakku said fuel accounts for 40 to 50 percent of an airline’s costs, and prices jumped from 2.10 dollars a gallon to between 4.40 and 4.50 dollars amid the situation in the Middle East.

Unlike many other countries that subsidized their national carriers, Sri Lankan Airlines did not take a fuel subsidy from the government and could not sharply raise ticket prices, he said.

“If by any chance that fuel price had stayed at 2.1 dollars per gallon, the airline would not have incurred this loss this year,” Kodithuwakku said.

To address the shortfall, the carrier introduced a separate fuel surcharge on upcoming ticket sales and aims to break even by the next season in March, noting that the airline still turned a profit in the first two months of this year.

Despite current fuel pressures, the airline has a strong revenue base with a 1-billion-dollar annual turnover—about one percent of gross domestic product and was comparable to a 23-aircraft operation.

Catering, ground handling, engineering, and cargo units all generate profits, while losses stem only from passenger operations where the airline faces competition, alongside past management issues, he said.

Last year, intervention by the former Chairman reduced overpriced aircraft lease rates from around 600,000 rupees a month to about 280,000 rupees, helping the carrier record a 4-million-dollar operational profit.

“We had signed aircraft lease agreements for huge prices that were not in the market. During last year, we resolved all of those,” Kodithuwakku said.

Management is now working to resolve costly engine service agreements, he added.

While the state must still pay off existing historical debts, the government aims to rebuild operations from zero so that, barring further global shocks, the airline no longer adds losses to the Treasury and taxpayers, Kodithuwakku said. (Colombo/Nov25/2026)

Sri Lanka’s electronics, spices export help cushion fall in garment, tea foreign buying

Thursday November 26, 2026
 2:06 pm

Thursday November 26, 2026
 2:06 pm

ECONOMYNEXT – A rise in export revenue from electrical and electronic components along with spices, has helped cushion Sri Lanka this year as the island nation’s top export, garments, and No. 1 agriculture commodity, tea, face lower global demand, official data showed.

Export revenue from electrical and electronic components jumped 59.6 percent to US$566.1 million in the first 10 months of this year compared to the same period last year.

Spices and concentrates also helped boost the export revenue this year. Sri Lanka witnessed a 35.4 percent rise in export revenue from them during the period, the data showed.

These two sectors helped offset the fall in export revenue from both apparel and textiles as well as tea.

Export revenue from apparel and textiles fell 5.5 percent to US$4.2 billion in the first 10 months, while revenue from tea fell 6.8 percent.

Officials have cited the Middle Eastern crisis-led lower demand for the fall.

Already the fall in the exports of tea and garments have led to revise down the 2026 export revenue target by 7.5 percent to US$18.5 billion from the earlier US$20 billion, government officials have said. (Colombo/November 26/2026)

Sri Lanka’s Janashakthi Finance meets central bank capital adequacy requirements

Thursday November 26, 2026
 2:00 pm

Thursday November 26, 2026
 2:00 pm

ECONOMYNEXT – Sri Lanka’s Janashakthi Finance, formerly Orient Finance, said it has achieved full compliance with the central bank’s minimum regulatory capital adequacy requirements following a capital boost and asset optimization.

The company said in a market filing that its Tier I Capital Adequacy Ratio stood at 8.60 percent as of November 15, edging above the regulatory minimum of 8.50 percent.

“The Board of Directors wish to inform the Colombo Stock Exchange that the Company currently is compliant with the minimum regulatory capital adequacy requirements prescribed by the Central Bank of Sri Lanka (CBSL).”

Its Total Capital Adequacy Ratio reached 12.85 percent, surpassing the 12.50 percent regulatory requirement.

The turnaround addresses a shortfall previously reported in Note No. 06 of the company’s unaudited interim financial statements for the period ended September 30.

To shore up its buffers, the central bank granted approval on November 10, for Janashakthi Finance to include 350 million rupees of unsecured, unlisted subordinated debt as eligible Tier II capital.

The company also undertook measures to optimize its risk-weighted assets, further lifting its ratios.

Janashakthi Finance added that it is currently obtaining certification from its external auditors for approximately 198 million rupees in interim profits recorded for the six months ended September 30.

Once certified and approved under regulatory guidelines, recognizing these profits as Tier I capital is expected to further strengthen its capital base. (Colombo/Nov26/2026)

APAC banks’ resilient fundamentals support ‘Neutral’ 2027 outlooks: Fitch

Thursday November 26, 2026
 12:00 pm

Thursday November 26, 2026
 12:00 pm

Fitch Ratings-Hong Kong/Sydney/Singapore: Fitch Ratings expects broadly sound banking-sector conditions across APAC in 2027, underpinning our predominantly ‘neutral’ sector outlooks. Steady asset quality should cushion the effect of moderating economic and loan growth in emerging markets, while higher interest rates generally support margins and earnings in developed markets.

The main exceptions are Japan, Hong Kong and the Philippines, to which we assign an ’improving’ sector outlook, while Australia is ’deteriorating’. 

Continued interest-rate normalization should support Japanese banks’ earnings; while easing commercial real-estate market pressures – alongside solid wealth-management income and cross-border activity – underpin Hong Kong’s improvement. The Philippines should rebound from weak economic growth and elevated consumer impairments in 2026. In contrast, higher-for-longer rates, softer employment, and competition are likely to increase impaired loans and pressure profitability in Australia.

China’s banking-sector performance should defy slower economic and loan growth, as a modest recovery in margins, stronger fee income and broadly stable credit costs support profitability, while Thailand should also see steady performance as banks gradually work through legacy problem loans. The sector outlook for both markets is ‘neutral’, after entering 2026 as ‘deteriorating’. India and Indonesia remain among the stronger-growth markets, with margins supported by double-digit loan growth and repricing, although higher credit costs will limit profitability upside.

Elsewhere, higher rates should generally support margins across developed markets, but may increasingly test debt-servicing capacity among vulnerable borrowers.

Downside risks include persistent inflation and tighter-for-longer monetary conditions, renewed geopolitical tensions, and weaker global growth. Softer AI-related demand could also weigh on several export-oriented developed markets. Nonetheless, resilient asset quality, generally adequate capital, and broadly stable profitability provide buffers against these risks.

The reports, “Asia-Pacific Developed Markets Banks Outlook 2027” and “Asia-Pacific Emerging Market Banks Outlook 2027”, can be found at www.fitchratings.com or by clicking on the links.

Sri Lanka rejects all bids at Treasury bill auction following rate hike

Thursday November 26, 2026
 10:54 am

Thursday November 26, 2026
 10:54 am

ECONOMYNEXT – Sri Lanka’s Public Debt Management Office (PDMO) has decided not to accept any bids received at the Treasury bill auction held on Wednesday, November 25.

“Pursuant to Gazette Notification No.2459/52 dated 24.10.2025 and section 8.2 of the Direction on Primary Issuance of Treasury Bills Ref: 08/21/005/097/001 issued by the Central Bank of Sri Lanka, the Issuer has decided not to accept any bids received under this auction. ”

The rejection comes amid upward pressure on secondary market bond yields and a recent tightening of monetary policy by the central bank, which raised its key policy rate and the Statutory Reserve Ratio (SRR) citing high inflation and credit growth, on Friday (20).

The issue offered 80 billion rupees in Treasury bills. 

Before the central bank’s rate hike, the 3-month, 6-month, and 12-month bill rates were quoted around 9.35 percent, 9.43 percent, and 9.95 percent, respectively. (Colombo/Nov26/2026)
