Bilateral trade and investment: SL eyes RCEP through ASEAN door

26 Nov 2026 | By Nethmi Rajawasam

Sri Lankan Ambassador to Indonesia Sashikala Premawardhane reiterated Sri Lanka’s intention to join the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP) – through its work as an ASEAN Sectoral Dialogue Partner – during an interview with Indonesian media platform Good News from Indonesia, published yesterday (25).
“We hope that we will get the support of the member countries to become a sectoral dialogue partner in the future,” Premawardhane said, noting Sri Lanka’s interest in the preferential free trade agreement that links the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) with Australia, New Zealand, Japan, South Korea, and China, among others, and which came into effect in January 2022.
Sri Lanka submitted a letter of intent to the ASEAN Secretariat in June 2023.
For Sri Lanka, gaining membership is likely to include reduced tariffs, simplified customs procedures, and potentially expanded market access into the 15 RCEP economies, including Australia, Brunei, Cambodia, China, Indonesia, Japan, South Korea, Laos, Malaysia, Myanmar, New Zealand, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, and Vietnam.
“As an ASEAN regional forum member from 2023 to 2025 we have undertaken a lot of work, one in disarmament and non-proliferation, the other in disaster relief, and the other in defense education. So in all three areas Sri Lanka has hosted intersectional meetings of the ASEAN regional forum in Colombo,” Premawardhane added.
She also noted the asymmetry of investment between Sri Lanka’s private sector investments into Indonesia and Indonesia’s investments into Sri Lanka. 
“We have about $ 1.3 billion in investments from Sri Lanka into Indonesia. But we don’t have so much Indonesian investments in Sri Lanka. So we would like to see more Indonesian investments going into Sri Lanka uh in the next few years. So that’s an invitation to investors from Indonesia.”
Premawardhena, speaking to media during previous engagements, has stated that while Sri Lanka’s private sector has invested over $ 1.3 billion in Indonesia, Indonesian investment in Sri Lanka remained at around $ 3.5 million in 2025.
