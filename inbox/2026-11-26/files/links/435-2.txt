Sri Lanka opens Rs 161.5 m national battery testing centre

25 Nov 2026 | By Sindy Fernando

Sri Lanka has established its first comprehensive national facility for testing modern battery technologies, introducing specialised testing capabilities for local manufacturers, importers and technology developers through an investment of approximately Rs 161.5 million, under the patronage of the Science and Technology Minister Prof Chrishantha Abeysena, according to the Department of Government Information (DGI).

The new national facility, the Centre for E-Mobility and Battery Technology, was officially inaugurated at the National Engineering Research and Development Centre of Sri Lanka (NERDC) on Monday (23).

The DGI stated that the centre is expected to benefit local manufacturers, importers, researchers and technology developers by improving access to specialised testing services, supporting domestic product development and enhancing local value addition. 

The facility was developed with the objective of expanding the country’s testing capabilities beyond conventional battery assessments to encompass modern battery technologies, including assessments of the performance, capacity, durability and safety of various types of batteries and battery systems, the DGI added.

According to the DGI, of the total investment, Rs 118 million has been provided through the Government's National Quality Infrastructure (NQI) Strengthening Project, while the remaining Rs 43.5 million has been contributed through NERDC's own funds. 

The facility also includes assessing battery performance under different environmental conditions and conducting tests related to electric vehicle charging equipment, among other specialised services, the DGI stated.

The initiative marks a significant step in strengthening Sri Lanka’s quality infrastructure and supporting the country’s transition towards electric mobility and renewable energy technologies, the DGI stated.
