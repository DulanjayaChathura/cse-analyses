Says tax concessions alone not enough to attract investors; land allocation must be sped up

Committee discusses placing State land under BOI or a specialised land bank

Unspent funds to go back to Treasury unless conditions such as opening of LCs are met

President Anura Kumara Dissanayake has pressed for faster allocation of land to investors, saying tax concessions alone are not enough to attract investment while the process of obtaining land takes about 18 months, the Parliament Secretariat said.

The President made the remarks at a meeting of the Ministerial Consultative Committee on Finance, Planning and Economic Development, held under his chairmanship at Parliament on Monday.

The Committee discussed identifying State-owned land and placing it under the purview of the Board of Investment (BOI) or a specialised land bank. Such land would then be allocated faster through a mechanism based on the scale of investment, the employment generated and the potential impact on the national economy.

The President also called for faster district-level development projects and more efficient management of public funds. He said ministries and institutions should prepare plans to implement their projects from the start of each financial year and review progress monthly.

President presses...

Funds unutilised at the end of the year can be carried forward only if specific conditions are met, such as the opening of letters of credit (LCs) for the relevant projects, he said. Otherwise, the allocations must be returned to the General Treasury and fresh allocations made in the following year’s Budget. He stressed the need to monitor progress at district level, based on the project report books provided to District Development Committees.

The Committee also discussed fertiliser subsidies for farmers and the management of the Maha season paddy harvest. It highlighted the need to clear paddy stocks remaining in storage before the next Maha harvest arrives, and considered converting existing stocks into rice for distribution through welfare programs, or using them for animal feed and other products.

Cryptocurrency regulation, the digitalisation of the insurance and transactions sectors, and VAT-related issues faced by cooperative societies were also discussed.

The meeting was attended by Labour Minister and Deputy Finance and Planning Minister Dr. Anil Jayantha, Economic Development Deputy Minister Nishantha Jayaweera, Members of Parliament, and officials of the Ministry of Finance, Planning and Economic Development.
