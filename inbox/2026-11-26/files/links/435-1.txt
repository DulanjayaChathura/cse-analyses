Sri Lanka spared as US targets major trading partners with new tariffs 

 Thursday, 26 November 2026 06:47 

 -    
 - {{hitsCtrl.values.hits}} 

Sri Lanka has been left out of the latest round of US tariff measures targeting several major trading partners, including China, India, the European Union (EU), Vietnam and Bangladesh.

The new tariffs, announced by the White House following investigations into the trade practices of 14 economies, are set to affect some of the US’ biggest trading partners, with rates ranging from 20% to as high as 40%.

China faces the steepest tariff rate of up to 40%, followed by Vietnam at up to 35%, while other affected economies face rates of 20% or 25%. However, the effective rates for some countries will be lower due to tariff caps agreed by US President Donald Trump.

The 14 economies covered by the investigations are China, India, the EU, Singapore, Switzerland, Indonesia, Malaysia, Cambodia, Thailand, South Korea, Vietnam, Taiwan, Bangladesh, Japan and India. Sri Lanka is not among those named.

The latest move follows the US Supreme Court ruling that Trump’s previously announced Liberation Day tariffs were illegal, prompting the administration to pursue alternative trade measures.

The investigations were launched in March under Section 301 of the US Trade Act, which examines whether foreign government policies and practices unfairly disadvantage American businesses, workers and consumers.

Trump has repeatedly used tariffs as a central plank of his trade policy, arguing they would encourage domestic manufacturing and create jobs. However, economists have warned that import duties could raise consumer prices, fuel inflation and weigh on global trade.
