Jan.-Oct. merchandise exports up 5.72% YoY to $ 12.01 b; services rise 7.2% YoY to $ 3.26 b

EDB Chief Mangala Wijesinghe projects growth of around 6.5%, below 8-10% original target amid Middle East-related cost pressures

US remains single largest market, followed by India and UK; EU remains single largest region

Sri Lanka’s export earnings reached a record $ 15.28 billion in the first months of 2026, up 6.03% year-on-year (YoY), supported by strong merchandise and services performance, while October also delivered the highest monthly export revenue on record at over $ 1.62 billion.

The latest data released by the Sri Lanka Export Development Board (EDB) yesterday showed October earnings grew by 11.57% YoY, with merchandise exports rising 9.65% YoY to over $ 1.26 billion, whilst the estimated service earnings in the month showed a growth of 18.87% YoY to $ 360.21 million. 

However, total export earnings in October declined 8.69% month-on-month (MoM) decline compared with September 2026. 

Cumulative exports during the first 10 months saw merchandise export earnings increase by 5.72% YoY to over $ 12.01 billion, whilst services exports during the same period were estimated to have surged by 7.2% to $ 3.26 billion. Services exports include sectors such as ICT/BPM, construction, financial services, and transport and logistics.

“I am confident that Sri Lanka will achieve its $ 18 billion export earnings target for 2026 and could potentially exceed it,” EDB Chairman and CEO Mangala Wijesinghe told the Daily FT. 

He assured that the encouraging performance in October reflects the dedication of exporters and the growing potential of Sri Lankan products and services in international markets, the country could even do beyond $ 18 billion. 

However, he acknowledged that the country was unlikely to achieve the targeted 8-10% growth, with annual growth expected to be around 6.5%, citing the impact of the Middle East tensions and its direct impact on overall costs.

Addressing the media, Wijesinghe said moving forward, the priority must be to strengthen export competitiveness, diversify products and markets, enhance value addition, and accelerate innovation and technology adoption to create greater opportunities for Sri Lankan businesses.

The EDB noted that the merchandise exports growth in October was driven largely by improved demand across the agricultural and industrial product categories, along with service sectors. 

Cumulative export earnings from key value-added sectors was supported by strong performances in Electrical and Electronic Components (EEC) with 59.60% YoY to $ 566.14 million, spices and concentrates rose by 35.35% to $ 498.43 million, rubber-based exports increased by 101.10% YoY to $ 873.85 million and processed food and beverages rose by 18.93% to $ 594.52 million during January–October 2026.

However, the apparel and textiles sector declined by 5.50% YoY to over $ 4.19 billion during January–October 2026, compared with the corresponding period in 2025. This decline was driven by a 5.16% reduction in apparel exports to $ 3.89 billion and a 9.70% decrease in textile exports to $ 299.26 million. 

Wijesinghe said the sector’s performance was affected by subdued demand in key export markets, particularly the US and the European Union (EU), which together account for more than half of Sri Lanka’s apparel exports.

Tea export earnings declined by 6.77% YoY to $ 1.2 billion during January–October 2026. The decline was mainly driven by lower earnings from tea packets and bulk tea, which decreased by 5.18% and 10.37%, respectively.

The US, Sri Lanka’s largest single export destination, accounting for around 21% of total merchandise exports, recorded YoY growth of 7.77% in October 2026, with export earnings reaching $ 265.32 million. Cumulative exports to the US during January–October 2026 also increased marginally by 0.81% YoY, reaching $ 2.51 billion. This performance reinforces the continued importance of the US market to Sri Lanka’s merchandise exports, maintaining its position as the country’s leading export destination.

India overtook the UK to become Sri Lanka’s second-largest export destination, with cumulative exports increasing 14.16% to $ 989.66 million during the first 10 months of 2026. October exports to India surged 34% YoY to $ 99.82 million. 

In contrast, exports to the UK fell 1.88% YoY to $ 69.04 million in October, while cumulative earnings declined 8.67% to $ 723.40 million.

Exports to the European Union (EU), which accounted for 21.57% of Sri Lanka’s total merchandise exports during January–October 2026, registered a YoY decline of 6.64% in October 2026, with export earnings amounting to $ 265.14 million. Cumulative exports to the EU during January–October 2026 also declined marginally by 0.85% YoY, reaching $ 2.59 billion.
