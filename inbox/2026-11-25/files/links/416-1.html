ADB approves $ 200 m loan to boost MSMEs

24 Nov 2026


The Asian Development Bank (ADB) has approved a $ 200 million loan facility to help Sri Lanka strengthen micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises (MSMEs) by expanding access to inclusive finance, reducing credit information gaps, and building resilience to shocks.

The financing package consists of a $ 100 million policy-based loan to improve the financial ecosystem for MSMEs and a $ 100 million financial intermediation loan to expand access to investment loans for MSMEs including $ 40 million for green loans.

“This programme will help the Government of Sri Lanka build a stronger, more inclusive financial ecosystem that enables MSMEs to grow, innovate, and withstand economic shocks and disasters related to the changing climate,” said ADB Country Director for Sri Lanka Shannon Cowlin. “By improving access to finance, especially for women entrepreneurs, and expanding green financing, ADB is supporting Sri Lanka’s transition to a more inclusive, resilient, and sustainable financing ecosystem for MSMEs.”

Sri Lanka’s MSME sector is crucial to the country’s economy, contributing more than 52% of gross domestic product and 45% of overall employment. However, they continue to be constrained by limited access to finance, low levels of digitalisation, and vulnerability to shocks such as disasters related to the changing climate.

The ‘Building a Strong and Inclusive Financial Ecosystem for MSMEs Sector Development’ programme will address these challenges by expanding financial inclusion through enhanced digitalisation, improving equitable access to information for MSMEs, formalising institutional processes, and enhancing financial literacy.

The programme will support legal reforms to establish the Microfinance and Credit Regulatory Authority, improve oversight and reduce predatory lending, strengthen credit information systems to reduce information gaps, expand credit guarantees, and advance digital financial infrastructure and consumer protection.

Credit lines will be provided by participating financial institutions to expand green financing for MSMEs engaged in environmentally sustainable investments and activities.

Further, the programme will also help MSMEs at least 20% of which are owned or led by women invest in measures to mitigate and adapt to shocks related to the changing climate, supporting innovative financing mechanisms to help growth-stage SMEs access equity financing.
