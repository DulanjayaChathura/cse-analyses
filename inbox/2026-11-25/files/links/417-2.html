Oil slumps to 3-month low on increased Middle East flows, Libyan pipeline restart

25 Nov 2026


Oil prices fell more than 3% on Monday to settle at a three-month low on rising supplies from the Middle East and the restart of ​a Libyan oil pipeline, which offset worries about shipping and refinery attacks in the region.

Brent crude futures settled down $ 3.20, or 3.46%, at $89.37 a barrel. ‌US West Texas Intermediate futures settled down $3.24, or 3.88%, at $80.17 a barrel.

Brent closed at its lowest point since 31 August, and WTI at its lowest since 10 August.

Libya's Sharara oil pipeline has reopened, and crude is beginning to flow into storage tanks at the Akakus facility in Zawiya, the chairman of ​the National Oil Corp told Reuters on Monday.

JPMorgan expects oil futures to fall to the high $70s in the first half ​of 2027 due to an estimated supply surplus, with higher Middle East production outside Iran next year along ⁠with more supply from the Americas led by the US.

Bank of America said geopolitics would remain the key driver of oil prices, with ​relatively low global inventories leaving much of the world vulnerable to supply disruptions.

"It is partly the growing signs of a glut, but ​with each passing day it seems less likely that Trump will attack again," said IG chief market analyst Chris Beauchamp.

On Sunday, however, the Iran-aligned Houthi movement said it attacked a Saudi Arabian oil refinery, while the UK Maritime Trade Operations reported multiple attacks on shipping in the Gulf and near the Strait of Hormuz over ​the weekend.

Bahrain said an Iranian attack hit a civilian industrial facility on Saturday, while Iran's Revolutionary Guards said they had launched a missile-and-drone assault ​on US military positions in the kingdom, home to the US Navy's 5th Fleet.

Elsewhere, Ukraine's military said on ‌Monday it ⁠had struck Russia's Lukoil oil refinery in Perm overnight, damaging three processing units.

The Kaleykino oil pumping station in Tatarstan was also hit, Ukraine's General Staff said on the Telegram messaging app, the second strike on that site in four days. Four reservoirs were damaged, it added.

Meanwhile, Russian oil exports from its main oil terminals in the Baltic and Black Sea face growing delays due to bad weather and persistent Ukrainian drone attacks, according to ​traders and people with direct knowledge ​of loading schedules and shipping ⁠data.

In Turkey, talks on Sunday at the global climate conference COP31 collapsed for the first time since 2009 after nations could not agree on a deal on including a statement on the ​phase-out of fossil fuels.

Saudi Arabia and other big oil-producing countries have said fossil fuels will play ​a role in the ⁠economy for the foreseeable future and they lobbied to keep any mention of fossil fuels out of the text, observers at the conference said.

OPEC+ ministers will hold a series of meetings on 29 November to decide their 2027 output policy. The group is made up of OPEC and ⁠other oil ​producers such as Russia.

Meanwhile, US crude oil and product inventories are expected to have ​risen by about 900,000 barrels in the week ended 20 November, a preliminary Reuters poll of five analysts showed on Monday.
