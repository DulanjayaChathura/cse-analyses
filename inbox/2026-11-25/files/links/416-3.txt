National inflation accelerated to 8.5% year-on-year (YoY) in October 2026 from 8.1% in September, as both food and non-food prices rose faster. The figures come from National Consumer Price Index (NCPI) data released by the Department of Census and Statistics (DCS) on Monday.

The reading ends a one-month pause in the climb. YoY inflation held at 8.1% in September after rising every month from 1.6% in February. A year earlier, in October 2025, it stood at 2.7%.

The NCPI (Base: 2021=100) rose to 225.1 points in October from 224.2 in September. The month-on-month (MoM) increase of 0.9 index points, or 0.41%, was the largest monthly rise since July. It corresponds to a rise of Rs. 466.07 in the expenditure value of the market basket, against Rs. 123.85 in September.

The 12-month moving average, which compares the average index over the past 12 months with that of the preceding 12 months, rose to 5% from 4.5%.

Food inflation rose to 7.2% YoY from 6.9%. Non-food inflation reversed its September easing and climbed to 9.5% from 9.1%. Non-food items contributed 5.29 percentage points to the headline rate, against 3.21 percentage points from food.

Transport remained the biggest single contributor, adding 2.45 percentage points, up from 2.20 percentage points in September. Petrol alone accounted for 1.21 percentage points, followed by Bus Fare (0.36), Diesel (0.20) and Transport for Schooling/Preschooling (0.19). 

Housing, Water, Electricity, Gas and Other Fuels added 1.17 percentage points, led by Housing Rent (0.46), Materials for Maintenance (0.30) and L.P. Gas (0.23). Restaurants and Hotels (0.52), Education (0.39) and Miscellaneous Goods and Services (0.26) also contributed.

On the food side, the annual increase was driven by Vegetables (0.79 points), Fresh Fish (0.47), Dried Fish (0.40), Big Onions (0.38) and Milk Powder (0.26). Declines in Coconuts (-0.48), Rice (-0.32) and Limes (-0.07) partly offset these gains.

On a MoM basis, non-food items added 0.33% to the index, led by Petrol (0.15%) and Tuition Fees (0.05%), while food items added 0.08%. Within food, increases in Vegetables (0.25%), Limes (0.20%) and Milk Powder (0.07%) were largely offset by lower prices for Fresh Fish (-0.27%) and Green Chillies (-0.16%).

The data come days after the Central Bank of Sri Lanka (CBSL) raised its Overnight Policy Rate (OPR) by 25 basis points to 9%. It was the second hike this year, after a 100 basis point increase in May. The Standing Deposit Facility Rate and the Standing Lending Facility Rate, which are linked to the OPR, rose to 8.50% and 9.50%, respectively. 

The CBSL also raised the Statutory Reserve Ratio (SRR) by one percentage point to 3%, effective from the reserve maintenance period beginning 9 December, 2026. The move permanently absorbs about Rs. 150 billion from the money market.

The CBSL’s target measure, Colombo Consumer Price Index (CCPI) inflation, rose to 8.3% in October. The CBSL projects it will reach about 9% by the end of November and stay elevated for several months. It now expects inflation to return to the 5% target in the second half of 2027, later than previously forecast. One-year-ahead inflation expectations have risen recently, while medium-term expectations remain anchored around the target, the CBSL said.

“In the short term, what we do currently may not have a significant impact on inflation, but it will have a small signalling effect and help anchor inflation expectations,” Central Bank Governor Dr. Nandalal Weerasinghe told the post-Monetary Policy Review media briefing last week.

Excess liquidity in the money market stands at about Rs. 450 billion, which leaves roughly Rs. 300 billion after the SRR increase. “This is a good opportunity for us to use this instrument to absorb liquidity on a permanent basis,” Dr. Weerasinghe said.

CBSL Senior Deputy Governor Dr. Chandranath Amarasekara said: “You can think of this as SRR normalisation.”

The CBSL said the easing cycle could resume once inflation is clearly on a downward path.
