Stock futures tumble as Nvidia leads AI sell-off, data shows inflation picking up: Live updates

Sean Conlon

Chloe Taylor

Lee Ying Shan

Sarah Min

Traders work on the floor of the New York Stock Exchange on November 23, 2026 in New York City. 
Spencer Platt | Getty Images

U.S. equity futures were lower on Wednesday as the artificial intelligence trade faced more pressure, and traders reacted to the latest inflation data and more U.S. tariffs.
Nasdaq-100 futures slumped 3%. Dow Jones Industrial Average futures dropped 635 points, or 1.3%, while S&P 500 futures fell 2%. 
Nvidia and Anthropic led AI-related stocks lower, with the former sliding 6% in the premarket and the latter dropping 4%. Oracle declined about 6%, and Alphabet shed 4%. The State Street Technology Select Sector ETF (XLK) traded more than 3% lower.
Dell Technologies fell more than 6% despite the company's strong third-quarter earnings and fourth-quarter guidance. Meanwhile, semiconductor stock Ambarella plummeted more than 30% after its forecast for the current quarter came in light. The company's earnings and revenue for the third quarter also missed expectations.
Investors digested the latest personal consumption expenditures price index reading, which showed that inflation still isn't slowing down. Headline PCE rose 0.3% in October, putting the annual rate at 3.8%. Both figures were in line with expectations. Excluding food and energy prices, core PCE increased 3.3% year over year, coming in slightly above the Dow Jones forecast.
The data likely increases the chances of more rate hikes from the Federal Reserve, putting investors in a risk-off mood.
Investor sentiment was also weighed by the White House unveiling a barrage of new tariffs on more than a dozen major trading partners, including China and the European Union.
Wall Street is coming off a losing session for the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite, after tech stocks gave back some of their gains from the prior session as Nvidia sank and Anthropic fell below its IPO price. The broader index fell 0.5% and the Nasdaq slid 1.5%. On the other hand, the 30-stock Dow, which has less exposure to tech, climbed more than 400 points, or 0.9%. 
Tuesday's choppy trading action was just the latest in a volatile month. Even before the ouster and reinstatement of Nvidia's Jensen Huang, and Democrats' sweep in the midterm elections, investors were deliberating the future of the artificial intelligence build-out amid a worsening macroeconomic outlook. 
"Let's remember that the sell-off in the AI ecosystem predated the news about Huang and Nvidia," Liz Ann Sonders, chief investment strategist at Schwab Center for Financial Research, told CNBC's "Closing Bell" on Tuesday. 
"The concerns about capex, the data center build-out, the spread between the cost of capital and return on invested capital, the bifurcation that's happening in the market now between sort of profitable segments of the market and unprofitable," she added. "I think that is the bigger, more aggregate story." 
In Asia, South Korea's Kospi slumped over 6% on Wednesday, while Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 2.88%. Hong Kong's Hang Seng Index lost 0.13%, while mainland China's CSI 300 lost 1.49%. Australia's S&P/ASX 200 was down 0.39%.
European shares traded broadly higher on Wednesday, with the pan-European Stoxx 600 index adding around 0.3%. However, regional tech stocks fell as the global sell-off of semiconductor and AI-adjacent assets continued. 
In a note on Wednesday morning, Deutsche Bank's Jim Reid said a "risk-off tone" had returned to the market over the last 24 hours, adding: "It's pretty wild out there."
"If you're looking for good news, we're back to the rotation trade rather than the indiscriminate sell-off we saw towards the end of last week," he said, pointing to rallies in defensive sectors like healthcare and consumer staples. 

8 Min Ago
PCE rises 3.8% in October

The personal consumption expenditures price index increased by 3.8% in October from the year-earlier period, up from 3.6% in the prior month — a sign that inflation persists. That's in line with a Dow Jones forecast. 
Excluding food and energy, PCE advanced 3.3%, slightly above a 3.2% consensus. 
— Fred Imbert

21 Min Ago
U.S. proposes new tariffs, including against China and EU

The U.S. proposed on Wednesday a slew of new tariffs on 14 trading partners, including China, the European Union and Japan. 
A senior U.S. official said all of the trading partners have been informed of the new levies, and that neither country's tariffs will exceed rates announced as part of framework deals on reciprocal trade. The new tariffs will not stack on top of existing duties, except for China, which faces a 20% tariff on top of the 25% imposed in 2018 over forced technology transfers.
The Trump administration says the new measures are to combat "overcapacity," where a government subsidizes excess industrial production, allowing cheaper goods to enter the U.S. market and undermine domestic producers. 
Tariffs announced on Wednesday are being implemented under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974. In February, the U.S. Supreme Court struck down tariffs that the Trump administration had implemented under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act.
— Lim Hui Jie and Davis Giangiulio

4 Hours Ago
Swiss lawmakers advance plan for tougher UBS capital rules

The UBS Group AG headquarters in Zurich, Switzerland, on Wednesday, July 30, 2025.
Bloomberg | Bloomberg | Getty Images

A Swiss parliamentary committee has backed tougher capital rules for UBS, moving a proposal that the bank says would require it to hold about $16 billion in additional core capital one step closer to becoming law.

The lower house's economics committee endorsed a plan that would require UBS's Swiss parent bank to hold tier 1 capital equal to 90% of the book value of its stakes in foreign subsidiaries. It matches a plan backed by Switzerland's upper house in September — but falls short of the Swiss government's proposed 100% requirement.

UBS.

The plans stem from UBS's emergency takeover of stricken rival Credit Suisse in 2023, with lawmakers aiming to make the bank more able to absorb potential losses abroad. UBS has resisted the move.
"If confirmed at the conclusion of the ongoing parliamentary process, today's recommendation by the committee would result in a further excessive tightening of Swiss capital requirements, which are already among the most stringent globally," UBS said in a statement.
The full lower house is expected to take up the bill in December. UBS shares were almost 1.2% higher in morning trade.
— Hugh Leask

6 Hours Ago
French consumer confidence ‘remains weak’ as unrest continues

MARSEILLE, FRANCE - NOVEMBER 7: People gather in Reformes Square prior marching towards the Porte d'Aix on November 7, 2026 in Marseille, France. Protests over deteriorating public services and the cost of living continue across France, with demonstrators taking to the streets for a fourth consecutive weekend. The protests were initially sparked by a student campaign to blockade schools over dissatisfaction with conditions in the French education system. The previous demonstrations, although largely peaceful, have seen thousands arrested and some incidents of violence. (Photo by Sener Yilmaz Aslan/Getty Images)
Sener Yilmaz Aslan | Getty Images News | Getty Images

Consumer confidence "remained weak" in France this month, the country's statistics agency Insee said on Wednesday. 
While Insee's confidence index rose marginally, analysts pointed out that it remained well below its long-term average. 
French consumer confidence has fallen sharply since the U.S.-Iran war began and sparked a resurgence of inflation. It fell further from September, when French student protests began and evolved into a nationwide series of protests, riots and strikes.

6 Hours Ago
‘Indiscriminate sell-off’ becoming a rotation trade, Deutsche Bank says

In a note on Wednesday morning, Deutsche Bank's Jim Reid said a "risk-off tone" had returned to the market over the last 25 hours, adding: "It's pretty wild out there."
"If you're looking for good news, we're back to the rotation trade rather than the indiscriminate sell-off we saw towards the end of last week," he said.
While he noted that AI-exposed assets like the Philadelphia semiconductor index posted sharp declines in Tuesday's session, and a tech sell-off had pulled various indexes lower into Asian trading on Wednesday, Reid said "broader equities [had] held up comparatively well."
"Clearly the latest declines have revived questions as to whether this selloff has further to run," he said. "But so far at least, the current moves don't have any of the characteristics that accompanied the outright bear markets of recent decades."
— Chloe Taylor

6 Hours Ago
German economy grew 0.1% in third quarter, weaker than first thought

Shoppers at Roemerberg square in Frankfurt, Germany, on Wednesday, Aug. 12, 2026. 
Ben Kilb | Bloomberg | Getty Images

Germany's economy grew by just 0.1% in the third quarter of this year, final data from the country's statistics agency showed on Wednesday. 
It marked a downward revision from preliminary data published last month, which estimated quarter-on-quarter GDP growth of 0.2%. 
Officials said exports and retail sales had performed weaker than initially anticipated. 
Europe's biggest economy grew by 0.4% in the first quarter of this year, and 0.3% in the second quarter. 
— Chloe Taylor

6 Hours Ago
Nearly half of European industrial firms in 'financial stress or distress': McKinsey

A metalworker grinding a peace of metal is pictured in a forge in Klitten, Germany. Manufacturing activity has struggled this year.
Florian Gaertner | Photothek | Getty Images

Data published by McKinsey on Wednesday showed 45% of Europe's industrial companies are showing "heightened levels of financial stress or even distress."
Analysts at the consultancy giant analyzed an index of around 1,200 public and private European companies with revenue of more than 100 million euros ($109.4 million).
"Industrial companies in Europe are a backbone of the region's economy, accounting for about 36 percent of value added, 29 percent of jobs, and more than 45 percent of R&D," the authors of a report on the findings said. "Yet this industrial engine is struggling to maintain its competitive position against other countries, particularly China."
But they noted that "decline is not inevitable" if companies enlist "a balanced set of actions taken in parallel." The report's authors pointed to strategies including raising labor productivity, addressing wasteful spending, using AI to reduce administrative burdens, and consolidating portfolios.
— Chloe Taylor

8 Hours Ago
‘Ready for any storm’: UAE minister optimistic on economy as IMF lifts forecast

The UAE is "very optimistic" about achieving 7%-8% growth next year, the country's economy and tourism minister Abdulla bin Touq Al Marri told CNBC after the latest data from the International Monetary Fund showed the country would avoid a recession in 2026.
The IMF last week revised the UAE's real GDP forecast to 0.2% growth in 2026 from a 3.9% contraction predicted a month ago. "The UAE is coming back, and that adjustment within one month shows that there is a huge potential aspect when it comes towards the end of the year," the minister told CNBC's Dan Murphy.
He said the tourism industry was already picking up with occupancy rates returning to "almost near normal" and international carriers including British Airways and Air France rerouting flights to the UAE. The minister expects economic growth to be higher than 0.2% this year.
The Iran war has disrupted economic activity in the UAE, with missile and drone attacks from Tehran and maritime disruptions weighing on energy trade, tourism, aviation and private-sector confidence.
Earlier this month, U.S. President Donald Trump said that renewed attacks on Iran were "highly unlikely" if negotiations produce a "very strong agreement." However, on Monday, Trump threatened that Tehran could face fresh U.S. strikes next year if the two countries failed to strike a deal, as per Fox News.
"We can we cannot promise the world for any storms that will land on our shore, but we can always promise that the UAE's economy is ready for any storm," Al Marri said.
—Priyanka Salve

10 Hours Ago
Trump open to pardoning Jensen Huang if needed: NY Post

President Donald Trump said in an interview with the New York Post that he would be willing to pardon Jensen Huang if needed after the reinstated Nvidia CEO appears before Congress next week.
Huang's reinstatement followed Trump's intervention with Nvidia's board, including a reported call into a board meeting to argue for his return. Even so, lawmakers are still set to scrutinize his relationship with suspected Chinese intelligence asset Alice Huang in two appearances next week.
Nvidia shares surged 31% on Monday after Huang was reinstated as CEO, before falling 5.9% on Tuesday amid a broader market pullback.
Trump also said that Huang should not need to invoke the Fifth Amendment to avoid self-incrimination when he appears for a closed-door interview with the House Foreign Affairs Committee on Dec. 1 and before the Senate Banking Committee on Dec. 3.
Trump added that while a pardon was "always an option," he did not think it would be "necessary," the newspaper reported. The president's clemency authority is limited to federal offenses, according to the Justice Department's Office of the Pardon Attorney.
—Shikhar Gupta

10 Hours Ago
U.S. jury convicts China's Huawei over fraud, conspiracy to steal trade secrets

Chinese technology giant Huawei was convicted Tuesday in New York on multiple federal criminal charges, including fraud, U.S. sanctions violations and conspiracy to steal trade secrets, following a three-month trial, according to the U.S. Department of Justice.
The verdict marks a major development in a long-running criminal case against Huawei, with charges first publicly disclosed in 2019.
A federal jury found Huawei and three of its subsidiaries guilty of multiple offenses, including bank and wire fraud and conspiracy to commit money laundering, the Justice Department said in a statement.
"No person or company is above the law," U.S. Attorney General Todd Blanche said, accusing Huawei and its subsidiaries of conducting business in the United States through deception and theft.
The Justice Department said Huawei misrepresented its activities in Iran to banks to secure billions of dollars and retain access to U.S. dollar-clearing services. The company also supplied surveillance cameras that enabled Iranian authorities to monitor protests, the department said.
—Jenny Lee
