Parliament panel calls for EV tax incentives, battery recycling rules

Parliament’s Committee on Ways and Means has called for tax concessions and policy incentives to promote electric vehicles (EVs), along with a proper system to manage used EV batteries, the Parliament Secretariat said.

The Committee met last week under the chairmanship of Committee on Ways and Means Chairman MP Wijesiri Basnayake. It discussed vehicle imports and the taxes on them, the promotion of EVs, and delays in clearing imported goods through Customs.

Members stressed the need to simplify tax policies so that EVs become affordable to the general public. The Committee also discussed the need to update Customs classifications and Harmonised System (HS) codes, the codes used to classify goods for Customs duty, in line with international standards, and to introduce proper quality standards for EVs and their batteries.

The Committee paid special attention to the absence of a proper system in the country for disposing of or recycling used EV batteries. Officials said importers or manufacturers should be made responsible for the proper disposal or recycling of batteries once they reach the end of their useful life.

To reduce the impact of EV charging on the national electricity grid, the Committee also discussed encouraging the use of solar power and battery energy storage systems.

On vehicle imports more broadly, the Committee examined the number and value of vehicles imported since import restrictions were relaxed, and the tax system that applies to them. It discussed the complexity of calculating taxes on imported vehicles, which depend on several factors including engine capacity, Cost, Insurance and Freight (CIF) value, and excise duty, as well as issues relating to Customs valuations.

The Committee also discussed delays in clearing imported goods caused by the need to obtain approvals from several Government institutions besides Customs. The lack of a common digital system among these institutions, including the Department of Import and Export Control, has led to delays in approvals and difficulties in coordination.

Officials said the delays result in containers being held at ports, forcing importers to bear additional costs, including storage charges. The meeting emphasised the need to strengthen coordination among the institutions involved and speed up the clearance process.

Deputy Ministers Eranga Weeraratne and Nishantha Jayaweera, MPs Ashoka Sapumal Ranwala, Dr. Nandana Millagala and Champika Hettiarachchi, and officials of Sri Lanka Customs attended the meeting.
